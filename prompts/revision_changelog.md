## Method

Each modified frame received the **full character lock block verbatim** — no
shorthand — inserted directly after the front style anchor, followed by a
placement-and-reaction clause written for that beat, then the original scene
description unchanged.

Frames that read `no characters in the scene`, `no human characters in the
scene` or `no faces in the scene` had that clause removed where the character
was added; leaving it would put a direct contradiction in the prompt. That is
the only deletion either pass made, and it is logged per frame above.

Constraints held, verified programmatically across all 157 frames:

- Front style anchor present: **157 / 157**.
- End style lock present and terminal: **157 / 157**.
- One frame per line, blank line between entries: **0 multi-line entries**.
- File ends with a single newline, no trailing blank.
- The 127 unmodified frames: **byte-identical** between base and v2.
- Every modified frame keeps its original scene text intact after the injected
  clause, except the logged exclusion-clause removal. **0 integrity problems.**
- Supporting characters — Skinner, Schultz, Fiorillo, Tobler, Sapolsky, Schüll,
  Harris, Gloria Mark — keep their own distinct descriptions and were not
  altered. Neither were the prehistoric-variant frames, which already carry the
  character in his hide wrap.

## Placement rationale

The protagonist is a **reacting observer**, never a second explainer. He is
placed at a frame edge or in the lower foreground, scaled small against the
subject, and given one readable expression drawn from the vocabulary already in
the file — the same raised-brow tilt, uneasy frown, and wide-eyed realisation
the hook frames use. No new expression vocabulary was invented.

Four placements do structural work rather than filling a gap:

- **`[02:02]`** and **`[04:11]`** — he stands at the base of a bar chart so the
  tall bar is scaled against a known height instead of floating.
- **`[02:22]`** — his far glove drifts toward his pocket without him looking,
  the exact gesture from `[00:00]`. It closes the Skinner run by rhyming the
  lever with the phone.
- **`[07:05]`** — he kneels at the pull-to-refresh frame and notices the slot
  lever overlaid on his own gesture. That rhyme is the segment's thesis.
- **`[08:26]`** — he stands at the foot of the enormous "47", which both scales
  the number and puts the episode's hardest datum on a face.
